Posted by the amber Team

  • 17 Nov: In #4 USA, the team shared news of a US court ruling on F-1 student visa stay rules, with a link to the source. Members responded positively.
  • 16 to 23 Nov: Admins removed spam and unwanted posts in #2, #3, #4 and #5.

Headline Takeaways

  • Community #5 is now the heart of the community: 258 of 361 messages (71%) and +290 members. Its UK group alone had 223 messages.
  • Peer help in #5 UK: members explained UK entry marks, MOI letters vs IELTS or Duolingo, January intake options and scholarships for bachelor's vs master's.
  • Community #4 went quiet: 60 messages, down from 280. Its biggest moment was the team's US visa news post.
  • Amber Scholar applicants are waiting: in #3 USA, members asked whether anyone has heard back or reached a "second stage", and some couldn't find the link.
  • Short.io: about 393 clicks in the week of 15 to 21 Nov (+36%). /community-E drove 90% of November's clicks, and 197 clicks this month came from Gmail.
  • GA4: 40 new users in the week of 16 to 22 Nov, up 38%.
Needs Attention
  • Agent recruiting in #5: an "Education Consultant" account posted in Australia, Canada and Germany offering to help with study-abroad questions, and members replied "I'm interested".
  • Fraud warning in #4: a member warned that a phone number shared in the groups is a fraud and asked everyone to report it; admins removed the posts.
  • Amber Scholar updates: applicants are asking about results and a "second stage". A short update on timelines (results by 31 January) would reassure them.
  • Unsolicited posts: the Leeds room-for-rent listing is back in #1 UK.

What this shows
  • Reach is limited by delivery: only about half of the links are delivered (47% this week), so most students never see the message.
  • Clicks stay small but are getting more efficient: 28 clicks in 7 days vs 30 in about 20 days, and the click rate on read messages rose from 4.6% to 6.2%.
  • The audience changed: this week 89% of recipients had never engaged with the bot (1,555 of 1,749), compared with about half in the previous period.
